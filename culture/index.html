Culture — ჭიდაობა

More than wrestling. A way of life.

Chidaoba is Georgia’s traditional jacket wrestling — a contest of grip and balance, fought in an open-air circle to the sound of zurna and doli.

01 — What is Chidaoba

Georgian jacket wrestling.

Two wrestlers, each wearing a chokha — the traditional Georgian coat. Each tries to unbalance and throw the other, and the jacket itself is what they grip.

But Chidaoba is not only a contest. UNESCO describes it as a complex practice that combines wrestling, music, dance and special garments, carried on in every region of Georgia — by village youth, city residents, sports clubs and schools.

Its code of conduct is chivalric. Georgians recognise it as a symbol of their identity, and it runs through the country’s folklore, art, literature and cinema.

02 — History & heritage

From combat to celebration.

How a fighting skill became one of Georgia’s most loved traditions — and kept its place between generations.

  1. 01

    A combat skill

    Chidaoba began as preparation for fighting. UNESCO records that it kept a combat function until the late Middle Ages. Contemporary reporting describes it as part of the training of Georgia’s army in the 13th century.

    Sources: UNESCO; AFP (2018)
  2. 02

    A spectacle for the community

    Gradually it became a spectacular sport. Tournaments turned into festive events in villages and towns — an open-air arena, a large audience around it, and music to open the bout.

    Source: UNESCO
  3. 03

    A code of chivalry

    Chidaoba carries a chivalric code of conduct. Descriptions of its techniques, and of codes of chivalry for champions, are reported to appear in old Georgian manuscripts.

    Sources: UNESCO; AFP (2018)
  4. 04

    Passed from hand to hand

    Skills pass from generation to generation — within families, in specialised schools and in village clubs. Young people learn by watching matches, then practise outdoors from early spring to autumn.

    Sources: UNESCO; AFP (2018)
  5. 05

    Recognised

    Chidaoba has been on Georgia’s Intangible Cultural Heritage State Register since 2011. In 2018 UNESCO inscribed it on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

    Source: UNESCO Decision 13.COM 10.b.15

03 — The art of wrestling

≈200Holds and counter-holds
UNESCO’s documented estimate

Grip. Balance. Timing.

In Chidaoba the jacket is the handle. Wrestlers fight for a grip on the chokha, break their opponent’s balance and throw.

What makes it an art is the combining. With an estimated 200 holds and counter-holds, a wrestler has to read the opponent, choose a combination and commit in an instant. UNESCO’s committee singles this out: how resourceful wrestlers must be in combining different holds and making quick decisions.

01

Grip

Everything starts with the jacket. Whoever controls the grip controls the exchange.

02

Balance

Standing wrestling: the aim is to take the opponent off their base and down.

03

Timing

A hold works in one moment and fails a second later. Counter-holds punish the wrong moment.

04

Creativity

Combining holds into something the opponent hasn’t seen — the quality UNESCO highlights.

Modern rules

As reported in 2018 — today’s competition rules, not historical ones.

  • A match lasts five minutes.
  • Intentional kicks and offensive blows are forbidden.
  • No holds on an opponent who has been brought down.
  • No creeping up on an opponent kneeling in a defensive position.

04 — Music & movement

The music starts the bout.

A Chidaoba match is never silent. The zurna and the doli mark its beginning, and their music drives the contest on.

The audience stands in a ring around the open-air arena. Wrestlers compete through special holds while the music lifts the dynamics of the fight — and when it’s over, they sometimes leave the arena with a Georgian folk dance.

ZurnaზურნაA wind instrument with a piercing, carrying sound.
DoliდოლიThe Georgian drum that sets the rhythm.

05 — Traditional clothing

ჩოხა

The jacket is the point.

UNESCO lists special garments — the chokha — among the core elements of Chidaoba, alongside the wrestling, music and dance.

In the bout, wrestlers wear high-necked woollen coats. The garment is what makes Chidaoba jacket wrestling: it is there to be gripped, and the fight for the grip on it decides the exchange.

Costume-making is one of the accompanying traditions that Georgia’s safeguarding plan for Chidaoba sets out to promote, together with its music and dance.

06 — The culture today

A living tradition.

Chidaoba is not a museum piece. There are wrestling sections in almost every village and city in Georgia.

From early spring to autumn, young people practise outdoors, putting into practice what they have learned by watching matches. The tradition is carried by youth, city residents, sports clubs, schools and amateur organisations.

In every communityWrestling sections in almost every village and city — and, as reported, a Chidaoba club in almost every small village.
Into the schoolsGeorgia’s safeguarding plan includes integrating wrestling into education and providing coaching within communities.
For the futurePlanned measures also include better infrastructure for tournaments and a specialised museum devoted to Chidaoba.
